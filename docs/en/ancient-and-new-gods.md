# Ancient & New Gods

**Ancient & New Gods** brings together Powers rooted in old beliefs and others born from the modern world.

Some have existed for millennia. Others appeared with modern societies, technologies, and obsessions.

## Powers

A Power may be an Egyptian, Norse, Greek, or other traditional deity.

It may also be far more recent: **Money**, **Communication**, **Networks**, **Consumption**, **Nation**, **Celebrity**, **Artificial Intelligence**, or any other force present enough in the world to become a Power within the Setting.

An ancient deity is not necessarily dying, and a new Power is not necessarily dominant. Some adapt, others refuse to change, some cooperate, and others compete over territories, followers, symbols, or uses.

## Agents

Agents may be avatars, children or descendants of a deity, heroes, chosen ones, priests invested with real power, incarnated fragments, or people transformed by their relationship with a Power.

A recent Power may likewise produce Agents in its own image: spokespersons, influencers, entrepreneurs, programmers, celebrities, activists, or people whose lives it has profoundly changed.

Their profession is not what makes them Agents. What matters is their real bond with the Power and the possibilities that bond opens in the fiction.

## Situations

A campaign may involve, among other things:

- the return of a forgotten deity;
- the birth of a new Power;
- competition between ancient and new gods;
- an unlikely alliance between Powers of different generations;
- the search for new followers or new forms of existence;
- an Agent who no longer recognizes the Power they serve;
- an ancient Power discovering that the modern world offers it new possibilities;
- a modern Power confronted with something far older than itself.

:::note[Example]

An Egyptian deity does not need to live as it did three thousand years ago. Its Agents may use social media, news media, finance, or contemporary politics.

Conversely, a Power such as **Communication** may discover that myths, rites, and symbols are technologies of transmission far older than it imagined.

:::
